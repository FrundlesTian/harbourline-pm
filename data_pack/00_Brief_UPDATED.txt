      From: Daniel Koh, Director, Estate Development, Harbourline Town Council Subject:
      Harbourline Community Hub Refurbishment — project brief

      Background. Harbourline Community Hub is a four-storey building completed in
      1994, about 9,800 m² gross floor area. It houses a sports hall, multi-purpose rooms, a
      tuition centre and a small food court, all on leases or licences. Usage has fallen, the
      M&E plant is at end of life, and residents have asked for childcare and senior services
      closer to home.

      Objectives. Refurbish the Hub into an integrated community facility that serves
      residents of all ages, improves accessibility and energy performance, and becomes
      the estate's flagship shared space.

      Scope. The refurbished Hub will include:

           an upgraded multi-purpose sports hall
           a new early childhood centre for about 100 children, run by our selected operator,
           Little Harbour Learning
           an active ageing centre, operator to be appointed
           a community kitchen and food court refresh
           a rooftop community garden
           full barrier-free access, new lifts and replacement of M&E systems

      Budget and time. Approved budget is S$18.5 million. Sixty per cent is funded by a
      central grant released in tranches against milestones; grant conditions will follow.
      Works are to complete within 22 months, with the Hub fully open by the fourth
      quarter of 2028.

      Delivery. We intend a single design-and-build contract. The Hub must remain open to
      residents throughout the works. Residents will be consulted on the design. Little
      Harbour Learning wishes to begin enrolment in month 18.

      Reporting. The Estate Development Committee meets weekly and expects a short
      status update. The grant body requires a monthly progress report. Our current
      tracking is in spreadsheets.

      Success. A fully operational Hub, delivered on budget, with high resident
      satisfaction.

  The five evaluation measures every team reports, so results can be compared across
  teams:

   1. Gap recall — how many of the brief's open questions your system surfaced
      unprompted.
   2. Invention rate — share of sampled claims in outputs that cannot be traced to the brief,
        the data pack or a sponsor answer.
   3. Re-run stability — what changes, and what duplicates, when the same input runs
      twice.
   4. Critic catch rate — faults you planted in a draft report that the critic found.
   5. Cost and time per full run — tokens or credits and minutes, as in Week 7.

  Rules
       AI use is expected, and must be declared. The design document lists which tools
       produced which parts of every deliverable, including the documents themselves.
       Academic integrity applies as in the course policy. Reusing another team's prompts,
       agents or evaluation results without attribution is collusion.
       Sponsor questions go through the sponsor channels only. Answers given to one team
       are posted to all teams.
       Synthetic data only. The data pack is fictional. Do not add real resident, employer or
       client information to any prompt, file or tool.
       Team problems are raised early. A member not contributing is raised with the
       instructor as soon as it happens, not first in the peer evaluation.
